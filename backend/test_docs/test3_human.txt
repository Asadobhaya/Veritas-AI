I remember the day I first stepped into the lab — nervous, excited, completely out of my depth. Dr. Rahman handed me a pipette and said, "Don't break it, those cost more than your tuition." I laughed, but he wasn't joking.

Over the next three years, I learned that research isn't glamorous. It's repetitive. It's frustrating. You run the same experiment forty times and get forty slightly different results. But sometimes — rarely — you see something nobody has seen before. That's the rush. That's why we do it.

My thesis explored the antimicrobial properties of silver nanoparticles synthesized from Azadirachta indica leaf extract. Sounds fancy, right? Really it meant spending months grinding leaves, mixing solutions, and staring at UV-Vis spectrophotometer readings until my eyes crossed.

Was it worth it? Absolutely. Would I do it again? Ask me after I finish writing this conclusion at 3 AM.
